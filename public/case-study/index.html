01 The opportunity

Pogo already has three of the four pieces.

Today a brand can use Pogo to find lapsed buyers and hear why they left. Then the trail goes cold: the brand runs a promotion somewhere else and has no clean way to know if it worked. Recall adds the missing step and turns a one-off study into an ongoing loop.

Research tells a brand what to do. Recall lets it do it, and shows the result in the same place.

Pogo's data foundation makes this possible. 3M+ opted-in US consumers, roughly 1 in 150 US shopping trips, and $470B+ in observed transactions. People connect cards, receipts and retailer accounts, and they already open the app to earn.

The platform already has the patterns. Signals sits in the left navigation. Studies, the AI agent, chart cards and respondent videos already exist. Recall reuses all of them rather than inventing a new product.

The market is moving toward closed loops. AI interview platforms are being valued as core infrastructure (Salesforce is reportedly in talks to buy Listen Labs). None of them can see purchases, so none of them can prove an outcome.

06 Design decisions

Choices that make it believable to a CPG buyer.

Built inside Pogo's own UI

Recall lives under Signals and reuses the platform's chart cards, study tabs, respondent quotes and agent chat. It reads as the next release, not a separate tool.

A holdout on every campaign

Insights teams distrust promo results that lack a control. A randomized holdout by default turns "it worked" into a lift number a finance team can accept.

Pay only on verified purchases

Members get cashback after their receipt is checked. Brands only pay for real returns, and members get a clear, fair reward.

"Observed" and "modeled" labeled apart

Every number says where it came from. Forecasts show ranges. This protects the trust that Pogo's "human truth" positioning depends on.

07 Business case

Why this matters for Pogo, not only for the brand.

Bigger budget

From research spend to promotion spend

Insights budgets pay for studies. Recall competes for the much larger trade and marketing budgets brands use to win customers back.

Recurring revenue

From projects to an always-on product

Lapse detection runs every week. Each brand gets a standing reason to log in, and Pogo gets a subscription instead of one-off studies.

Healthier panel

Members earn more for real behavior

Offers pay members for purchases they choose to make. That helps the payout complaints in Pogo's app reviews and keeps the people Pogo interviews engaged.

A pricing idea to test

A Recall subscription per brand that covers lapse detection and studies, plus a fee for each receipt-verified return. Pogo earns more when the campaign works, which is the clearest possible sales message. This is a hypothesis to test in the pilot, not a recommendation on price.

08 Risks

What could go wrong, and how to handle it.

Offers feel like spam to members

Cap offers per member per month, show only brands the member has bought before, and let members turn categories off.

High

Privacy and consent

Brands never see individuals. They see segments with minimum sizes, and offers go through Pogo. Members opt in to brand offers separately.

High

Small segments, noisy lift

Set a minimum segment size and holdout, and show confidence once the sample allows it. The prototype already avoids claiming significance.

Medium

Channel conflict with retailers

Position Recall as cashback that works at any retailer, so it adds to retailer media rather than competing with it.

Medium

Cashback payout reliability

Recall depends on fast, reliable payouts. Pair launch with the payout and support fixes raised in app reviews.

Medium
09 Next step

A six-week pilot with one existing CPG customer.

Start with a brand that already runs studies on Pogo, one lapsed segment, and one offer. Keep it small enough to run by hand where needed.
